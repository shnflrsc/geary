BulSU tracks class attendance closely, and showing up late or missing too many sessions carries fixed numeric cutoffs. Only students officially listed on the Management Information System (MIS) class roster may attend a class; sit-in students need prior permission from the academic unit head. During the first weeks of a semester, faculty exercise leniency and log attendance manually while enrollment lists stabilize.

## Late and absent cutoffs

- Arriving **15 minutes late** marks you tardy.
- Arriving **20 minutes late** marks you absent. Even when marked absent, your professor cannot bar you from entering the room to listen to the lecture.

## Wait times for a late instructor

Unless your professor sent notice ahead of time, your class may leave the room if the instructor has not arrived after:

| Class duration | Required wait time |
| --- | --- |
| 1-hour class | 15 minutes |
| 2-hour class | 30 minutes |
| 3-hour class | 45 minutes |

Walking out before the required wait time marks the class absent.

## Excused absences (30% cap)

Absences are excused only for 3 reasons:

- Official representation in approved curricular, co-curricular, or extra-curricular activities, such as varsity meets, press conferences, or cultural competitions
- Illness certified by an attending physician or the BulSU University Physician
- Force majeure, such as natural disasters or unavoidable emergencies

Even when an absence is excused, you still have to complete all required coursework, and total excused absences cannot exceed **30% of required semester attendance**.

## Unexcused absences and automatic FDA or UD (20% cap)

If you accumulate **more than 20% unexcused absences** in a subject **before midterm examinations**, the university drops you automatically from that class and records **FDA (Failure Due to Absences)** or **UD (Unofficially Dropped)** on your grade sheet.

An automatic FDA or UD counts against your academic standing under [What Happens When You Fail a Subject?](/articles/what-happens-when-you-fail-a-subject) and carries no fee refund under [How to Add, Change, or Drop Subjects](/articles/how-to-add-change-or-drop-subjects). If a serious illness or emergency will keep you out for weeks, notify the campus clinic early and review [How to File a Leave of Absence](/articles/how-to-file-a-leave-of-absence).

## Class suspensions, extreme heat, and online shifts

During typhoons, monsoon flooding, extreme heat indexes, or transport strikes, BulSU rarely cancels learning completely right away. Instead, the Office of the University President or the Office of the Chancellors issues campus-wide advisories that shift classes into 2 modes.

### Synchronous online learning

Synchronous online learning is common during extreme heat advisories, transport strikes, or initial storm warnings. Scheduled lectures move online; exams, presentations, or lab activities may continue in safe venues or be rescheduled depending on the advisory.

### Asynchronous online learning

Asynchronous online learning is common during prolonged Southwest Monsoon rains, widespread flooding, or government work suspensions. Under recent directives, faculty avoid synchronous sessions, hold off on graded deadlines requiring immediate submission, post review materials only, and extend leniency to students dealing with floods or power outages.

Student council petitions and Student Government advocacy posts calling for suspensions are requests, not official cancellations. Always wait for a signed university memorandum reposted on official BulSU or Student Government channels before skipping a scheduled session. If an instructor refuses academic consideration during an official flooding or weather advisory, report the issue through [How to File a Student Complaint](/articles/how-to-file-a-student-complaint).
