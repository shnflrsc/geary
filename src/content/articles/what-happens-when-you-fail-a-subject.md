Failing one subject at Bulacan State University triggers a formal warning, while multiple failures or drops in a single semester can trigger prerequisite locks, reduced loads, academic probation, or disqualification. Confirm with your College Dean if your college enforces stricter retention cutoffs.

## Progressive academic delinquency tiers

Each semester, your College Dean and unit head review failing grades (`5.00`) and drops under seven progressive tiers:

| Failures or drops in one term | Resulting standing | What happens next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 failing grade | Warning | You receive an official warning from your College Dean. |
| 2 dropped or failed subjects | Prerequisite block | You cannot enroll in next-term requisite subjects. With Dean approval, you may take minor subjects ahead within your curriculum unit cap. |
| 3 failed subjects (below 50% of load) | Academic Probation | You enter probation next semester and your unit head reduces your academic load proportionally. |
| On probation + 2 failures or drops | Dropped from course | You are removed from your current program. You may apply to shift to another program with acceptance from the receiving Dean and approval from the Vice President for Academic Affairs (VPAA). |
| 50% to 75% failed subjects | Suggested shift | BulSU advises you to shift to another program, subject to the receiving Dean's acceptance and VPAA approval. |
| More than 75% failed subjects | Disqualified | You are disqualified from continuing at BulSU. |
| On probation + 50% or more failed subjects | Disqualified | You are disqualified from continuing at BulSU. |

Two rules catch students off guard:

- **Drops count under two tiers:** Dropped subjects count alongside failing marks under the 2-subject prerequisite block and the probation 2-subject removal rule.
- **No residency extension:** Warning, probation, or shifting does not extend your maximum residency period (4 years for 2-year courses, 6 years for 4-year courses, 7.5 years for 5-year courses).

## Impact on graduation, honors, and scholarships

A past failing grade or irregular schedule does not stop you from graduating as long as you pass every required curriculum subject, clear all `INC` and `4.00` marks, settle clearances, resolve any disciplinary holds, and finish at least one year of residency before graduation.

Failing grades do affect awards and financial aid:

- **Latin Honors:** Requires no grade lower than `2.00` for Summa Cum Laude, `2.25` for Magna Cum Laude, or `2.50` for Cum Laude across all subjects.
- **President's List and Dean's List (Gold Gear Awards):** Require no grade lower than `2.0` across the last two consecutive semesters.
- **Student Assistantships and most scholarships:** Require zero failing or incomplete marks in the prior term.

## How to recover if you fall behind

- **Schedule a Guidance follow-up:** The Guidance and Counseling Services Center holds individual follow-up sessions for probationary students and students with low grades to help rebuild study habits.
- **Petition for a special class:** If a failed subject is not offered next semester, irregular students may petition for a special class (requiring at least 10 requesting students and Dean approval, following your college's semester petition window).

If you face dismissal from BulSU, the Office of the University Registrar still issues your transfer credentials once your clearance is complete.
